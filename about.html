About

I study the work around the technology

I am an independent evaluator based near Kyoto. My question is not whether AI or a robot can perform a task once. It is what people must understand, supervise and repair for that performance to remain useful.

A 17-year path into this question

Mass production taught me to distrust the isolated prototype

From 2009 to 2016, I worked on notebook and tablet programs from engineering changes through mass production. I learned that a design succeeds through interfaces among people, suppliers, tooling, schedules and evidence, not by component performance alone.

Robotics made the human system impossible to ignore

From 2018 to 2024, I worked across haptic hardware, tactile sensing and teleoperated retail robots. Field failures crossed electronics, software, manufacturing, operating conditions and human intervention. No single discipline owned the whole explanation.

AI shifted the question from making to judging

My software path began with a generic AI architecture contract in 2016 and now includes AI-assisted research, evaluation and product experiments. Building these systems made a recurring cost visible: the work required to verify an answer, recover context and keep a person in control.

What I do now

I turn that experience into fixed-scope written reviews for AI, robotics and other emerging interfaces. Hardware knowledge helps me see hidden dependencies; it is not the service identity. Accessibility helps me test whether information and control survive human variation; it is not a separate moral pitch.

I also serve as a founding board member of an Asia-Pacific inclusion association and build small tools that explore choice, evidence and human agency.

Working style

  • Independent and remote, based in Japan.
  • Asynchronous by default, with written reasoning and a defined end date.
  • Experience coordinating in English, Japanese and Traditional Chinese.
  • No implementation retainer or embedded team role.
