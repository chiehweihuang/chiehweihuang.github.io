What I evaluate

Robotics / Physical AI feasibility review

Whether the schedule, production path, supply chain, and engineering assumptions behind a hardware product can hold, before the money is committed.

Track record

Tokyo robotics, prototype to production (2017–2024)

  • Teleoperated robots deployed in Japanese retail (Telexistence): motor-driver boards, CAN safety architecture, field-failure investigation, an NVIDIA Jetson carrier-board program, and PCBA production management into 2024.
  • An open-source myoelectric prosthetic hand (HACKberry) and a haptic force-feedback exoskeleton (exiii): board design, then the assembly instructions and precision evaluations that turn a lab build into something a production line can repeat.
  • A powered prosthetic leg at a Tokyo prosthetics startup, through four engineering-validation stages: sensor-calibration tooling, battery and power-safety systems, and the design reviews that decided what was ready for the next build.
  • Board design for magnetic tactile skin sensors for robot grippers (XELA Robotics uSkin), and consumer health-device electronics with project-schedule control for a Tokyo hardware startup.

The recurring job was the same: judge which prototype was ready to advance, which process would survive scale, and why the thing that worked in the demo failed in the field.
