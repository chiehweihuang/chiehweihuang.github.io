The Physical AI Deployment Map

August 2026 · 16 deployment categories · positions are judgment, evidence grades are sourced · method

Where do robots actually work today? Each dot is a deployment category, placed by how open its operating environment is and how autonomous the system really runs. Color and shape show the grade of public evidence behind it. The pattern is the point: deployments crowd the constrained and the human-assisted regions, and the upper right, where every pitch deck points, is where evidence thins out.

  • A · Regulatory or independent data
  • B · Customer-confirmed
  • C · Vendor-claimed
  • D · Announcement-only
  • Dot size = deployment scale

Two readings honest people can disagree on: the skeptic sees an empty upper right; the optimist sees a ladder being climbed, and one A-grade dot (geofenced robotaxis) already living there. Both readings are on this chart. Deciding which dots move next, and which claims deserve your money, is evaluation work.

That gap is where human work becomes visible. My review asks what people must monitor, verify and repair before a system deserves the next commitment: see the Human-AI Collaboration Review, or chieh.wei.huang@gmail.com.

The data behind the map. Env = environment openness (0 constrained, 100 open world). Auto = autonomy (0 human-operated, 100 unattended).

Method

Compiled August 2026 from a 220-source review of public deployment reporting, regulatory filings, and teardown literature. Axis positions are my judgment as an evaluator; evidence grades follow the strongest public source found: A = regulatory or independent data, B = customer-confirmed deployment, C = vendor-claimed metrics, D = announcement or demo only. Dot positions for D-grade entries reflect the claimed operating point, not a demonstrated one. Disagree with a placement? Tell me, with a source, and I will move the dot.
